There is a time for telling

Just-in-time Telling
Core learning mechanism:
Students explore a problem or phenomenon before receiving the relevant
explanation.
Why it works:
People learn from expository materials by integrating the explanations with
their prior knowledge. But learners may not have sufficient prior knowledge to
do this meaningfully. Exploring the problem first helps develop the knowledge
that makes the subsequent explanation meaningful.
Example:
Exploring the doctor visit and balloon passage experiments before learning the
underlying memory concepts.

Contrasting Cases
Core learning mechanism:
Comparing examples that differ in important ways helps learners notice which
features and relationships matter.
Why it works:
People learn patterns in sensation by discerning what makes one thing
different from another. Contrasting cases juxtapose “near misses” to help
students pick out distinctive features.
Example:
The positive, negative, and near miss examples from the SPUC.
The different experiment examples in the doctor visit and balloon passage.

Undoing
Core learning mechanism:
Undoing is the process of identifying and replacing misconceptions and faulty
methods of reasoning.
Why it works:
Misconceptions and faulty reasoning can become entrenched and difficult to
detect. Undoing identifies the underlying problem and strengthens a
competing mental representation that can replace the erroneous one.
Example:
Ask learners to make a prediction on a task designed to reveal a common
misconception.

Self-Explanation
Core learning mechanism:
Learners explain new information to themselves rather than simply trying to
remember it.
Why it works:
Explanations cannot state every relevant connection between ideas. Selfexplanation prompts learners to fill in missing information and connections,
constructing a coherent understanding that goes beyond memorization.
Example:
Explaining why the patterns in the Balloon Passage experiments might occur.
