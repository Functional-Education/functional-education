Reward
Core learning mechanism:
Rewarding a behavior makes it more likely to be repeated; rewarding
successive approximations can shape new behavior.
Why it works:
Rewards can motivate learners to repeat rewarded behaviors in similar
situations. By reinforcing progressively closer approximations, a complex
target behavior can be built up gradually.
Example:
A math program gives students practice problems. After several correct
answers in a row, the learner unlocks new items for customizing their character.

Worked examples
Core learning mechanism:
Learners study a step-by-step demonstration of how to complete a procedural
task.
Why it works:
Worked examples let novices observe well-defined solution steps and expert
thinking. They reduce unnecessary search so learners can focus on the steps
and relationships that produce a solution.
Example:
To learn the Doomsday algorithm, students first observe a complete solution in
which the instructor demonstrates and explains each step of the method.

Deliberate Practice
Core learning mechanism:
Learners engage in focused and effortful practice to develop specific skills and
concepts beyond their current abilities.
Why it works:
Deliberate practice improves accuracy and fluency and makes skills less
effortful to execute. This frees cognitive resources for more complex tasks and
helps learners recognize meaningful patterns.
Example:
Practicing Doomsday problems with different century anchors and counting
directions.

Feedback
Core learning mechanism:
Feedback allows learners to sense the discrepancy between what they did and
what they should have done, enabling them to adjust future actions.
Why it works:
Learners need information about whether they are on the right track. Effective
feedback identifies the discrepancy and, ideally, points to what should be
changed or improved.
Example:
A student solves 0,4 ∙ 0,3 = 0.12. The system identifies the incorrect
placement of the decimal point and explains how to correct it.
