Scaffolding
Core idea:
Learners receive temporary support that enables them to engage in a task
they could not yet manage independently.
Why it works:
Scaffolding makes complex tasks manageable while keeping learners actively
engaged in the learning process. Support can be adjusted as learners develop
greater competence.
Two forms:
Structuring: helps learners manage complexity, focus attention, and organize
the task.
Problematizing: prompts learners to articulate their thinking, notice gaps, and
reconsider their reasoning.

Teaching
Core mechanism:
Teaching improves the teacher’s own knowledge.
Why it works:
Teaching requires learners to retrieve, organize, and explain their knowledge.
Questions from the learner can expose gaps and prompt further elaboration.
Example:
Two learners take turns teaching each other a concept they have just learned.
